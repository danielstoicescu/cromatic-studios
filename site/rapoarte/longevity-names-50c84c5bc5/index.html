A new candidate arrived after the vote closed, so it gets the same treatment ICONIS got. A fair reading, the register, the domains, and no thumb on the scale. The idea is one move: an eight, turned a quarter on its side, is the infinity sign. The name states the category and then refuses its arithmetic.

One glyph, three readings

Why an eight.

Reading 01The turn
Rotate the 8 ninety degrees and it becomes the infinity sign. Age stops being a count and becomes a line with no end. The whole brand promise sits in a quarter turn, which is also the entire logo brief.
Reading 02The loop
A figure eight is a path you can walk without stopping. That is the club's actual mechanics: the weekly circuit, the repeat booking, the habit that closes on itself. The shape describes the behaviour the business sells.
Reading 03The numeral
Eight is two full circles stacked: balance, completion, eight hours of sleep, and half the world's lucky number. A name with a numeral also hands the system a counting device for later. Rituals, rooms and blocks can carry it.
A note from our own method

We usually insist a mark comes out of the product, not out of a word trick. age8 bends that rule: the gesture lives in the glyph. What saves it is that the loop is also physically true. The training circuit is a closed path, so the shape points at the floor, not only at the typography. That is the argument to test, not to assume.

The honest ledger

For, and against.

What works
  • It says the category out loud. Every other candidate talked around longevity. This one puts age on the door and then overturns it, which is a more confident move than avoiding the word.
  • The identity is pre-solved. The mark is the name rotated. One gesture generates the logo, the animation, the pattern and the merch. Very few names arrive with their visual system already inside them.
  • Four characters. Reads at any size, fits any handle, survives a favicon, and types in one second. Operationally the lightest name in the whole process.
  • The register and the domains are smiling. No identical mark in sight on TMview and nine extensions open at pocket money prices. After the ReFrame reading, that is not a small argument.
  • No translation problem. Age is understood in Romanian without being Romanian, and the numeral is universal. It travels as far as the franchise ambition does.
Where the resistance is
  • Nobody knows how to say it yet. Age-eight in English, agé-opt in the Bucharest hybrid, or the intended age-infinit. A name people hesitate to pronounce loses word of mouth. The fix is to teach one spoken form from day one and never waver.
  • Said fast in English, it brushes against aged. For a longevity brand that is an ironic place to trip. Small risk, worth hearing out loud in the room before dismissing.
  • Age is the heaviest word in the category. It drags anti-ageing clinics and serums behind it. The 8 has to flip that reading instantly, every time, or the name lands in exactly the register the strategy avoids.
  • Alphanumerics read biotech. Age8 could be a Silicon Valley longevity startup. The Everyman warmth, the forty per cent that keeps this a club and not a protocol, has to come from everything around the name.
  • The 8-to-infinity turn is a known trick. It has been done, most famously on car badges. Ours earns its keep only if the execution is specific to this floor and this circuit.

A variant to weigh · not a third candidate

ag8.
The same turn, one letter shorter.

The quarter-turn 8 stays; the e goes. What is left in front of it is Ag, the chemical symbol for silver: the silver age, silver hair, a quiet nod to longevity without saying age. It works best under age8, as the monogram, the handle, the short mark on a stamp or a favicon. Or as the name itself, if the room wants the most compact form.

What works
  • Three characters. One fewer than age8, and the 8 still carries the whole logo brief.
  • The register is clean. A word search for AG8 across all classes returns nothing. The numbers are below.
  • A second layer. The silver reading is something age8 does not have: longevity hinted at, never stated.
Where the resistance is
  • The category leaves the door. Without the word age, the name reads like a code, A-G-8, until someone explains it.

  • Most domains are taken. .com, .net, .eu, .club, .one and .app are all registered. .ro and .fit are open.

One digit, three readings

Why the 8 sits at the end.

Reading 01The Romanian ear
Read the 8 the English way, as ate, and the word completes itself: longevitate. The international spelling carries a local password. Whoever gets it, smiles, and the audience that gets it is exactly the one walking through the door.
Reading 02The turn
The same quarter turn as age8: tip the digit and the count becomes the infinity sign. Here it lands at the end of the word, where the Y of longevity would have been, replacing the letter that closes the word with the sign that refuses to.
Reading 03The plain door
Nobody asks what the club is about. The category sits in full view, which costs nothing to teach and reads instantly on a façade, a map pin, a bag. The price of that clarity is paid at the trademark office, and the bill is itemised below.
A note from our own method

age8 and longevit8 are one idea at two lengths, so the room should judge them as a pair: the gesture alone, or the gesture inside the dictionary word. Compression against clarity. Our method leans toward the shorter form, because one strong shape beats a kit of parts, but the bilingual click of longevit-ate is a real asset the short form does not have. That tension is worth a vote, not a verdict from us.

The honest ledger

For, and against.

What works
  • The bilingual click. An English digit finishing a Romanian word is a genuinely original device, warm for locals and intriguing for everyone else. It gives the launch a story the press can retell in one sentence.
  • Zero education cost. The category is written on the door in full. No tagline needs to explain what the house does, which frees the tagline to do feeling instead of information.
  • It keeps the infinity move. The 8-to-∞ turn survives intact, now sitting where the word would have ended. The logo brief is still one gesture.
  • The exact register is empty. Four results on the precise string, all one unrelated mark family. Nothing identical anywhere in the EU set.
  • The domains are open, the .com aside. A separate check on 21 October confirmed .ro and .eu available, alongside .fit at €2.66 and the rest at cents. The .com has been registered since August 2026; who holds it, and whether it can be acquired, is still to be clarified.
Where the resistance is
  • One glyph from a mark that owns all three classes. LONGEVITY has been a registered EUTM in 41, 43 and 44 since 2008, and the holder refreshed its filing this June. The 8 has to carry the entire legal distance on its own, and that is a heavy load for one character.
  • The pronunciation forks three ways. Longevit-eight, longevit-opt, longevitate. The best reading is the intended one, but it must be taught, and until it lands the name wobbles in the mouth.
  • Digits inside words age badly. GR8, L8ER, SK8 carry an early-texting flavour that sits below the register this house is built for. The typography has to rescue it, every single time.
  • It leads with the category, not the feeling. The strategy sells how you look and feel now; longevity is the shelf, not the pitch. This name pitches the shelf. age8 has the same tension, but four characters wear it lightly where nine spell it out.
  • Nine characters. Long on a tote, long in a handle, long above a door. The compression that makes age8 feel inevitable is exactly what this form gives away.
